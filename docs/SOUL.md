# The Soul of Pocket Pro

What this product believes. Features change; this doesn't. Supplied and shaped by
the founder; every screen, lesson and line of copy should be able to point at a
sentence in this file.

## The mission

**Convert curiosity into commitment.** Take someone from "I'm interested in golf"
to "I have a practice routine, people to play with, and a reason to come back
next week."

## The mentor (not just a swing coach)

1. **Diagnose the person before the swing.** Goals, confidence, schedule, budget,
   athletic background, fears. A junior, a retired beginner, a college competitor
   and a new female golfer need different mentorship.
2. **Make golf less intimidating.** Etiquette, course behavior, practice habits,
   equipment, scoring, expectations — explained without making anyone feel
   ignorant. Especially for beginners and underrepresented golfers.
3. **Simplify improvement.** One or two clear priorities, never ten swing
   thoughts. The player leaves knowing exactly what to practice and why.
4. **Care about retention, not just performance.** Early wins: better contact,
   fewer lost balls, confidence on the course, a sense of belonging.
5. **Model the culture golf should have.** Patient, honest, inclusive, reliable,
   connected — introducing players to leagues, partners, events, fittings,
   junior programs, women's groups, competitive pathways.

## The etiquette doctrine

Golf etiquette is the set of habits that makes the game work when no referee is
standing over every shot: safety, pace, respect for people, care for the course,
and honesty.

1. **Be quiet and still when someone is hitting.** Silence matters most from
   setup through impact; stay out of their line of vision.
2. **Stand in the right place.** Never directly behind, directly in front, or too
   close — off to the side, outside swing and ball path.
3. **Keep pace.** Be ready on your turn; carry a few clubs when walking; read
   your putt while others prepare; let faster groups through.
4. **Respect the course.** Repair ball marks, replace or fill divots, rake
   bunkers, keep carts away from greens, tees and wet areas. Leave it better.
5. **Respect the green.** Never step on another player's line; mark your ball;
   stand still and out of sight; move to the next tee before writing scores.
6. **Be honest.** Count every stroke, penalties included. Don't improve your lie
   unless the format allows it. Unsure of a rule? Say so, handle it openly.
7. **Control your temper.** No thrown clubs, no sulking, no making the group
   manage your emotions. Golf is hard; lose with composure.
8. **Watch other players' shots.** Help track balls. If one might hit someone,
   yell "Fore!" immediately and loudly — don't wait to see.
9. **Know the order, don't be rigid.** Honors and farthest-first are tradition;
   ready golf keeps casual rounds moving. Whoever is safe and ready goes.
10. **Be gracious.** Compliment good shots. No unsolicited swing advice. Don't
    bring up someone's score or struggles. Help beginners without making them
    feel watched.

The refined part of golf is not snobbery — it is self-command. A polite golfer
is calm, punctual, honest, attentive, and considerate of the course and the
people sharing it.

## The honesty code (how we build)

- **Nothing fabricated.** Real courses, real photos, real credentials, real
  sources. Gaps are labeled, never painted over.
- **Draft until the pro approves.** Nothing ships in Tom's voice or name that he
  hasn't reviewed. His corrections are the product's training data.
- **We don't sell golfer data.** Secondary use is opt-in, separable, and never
  a condition of using the product.
- **OCR proposes, the golfer confirms.** The player is the authority on their
  own game; software drafts, humans decide.
- **Forecasts are forecasts, estimates are estimates.** Playability scores,
  financial projections and skill levels all carry their confidence honestly.
