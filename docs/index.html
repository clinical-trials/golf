What your pro actually says

You booked a driver lesson. Your rounds say the driver costs you a shot and a half. Three-putts cost you four. We're doing putting today.

Pocket Pro

One PGA Master Professional's method, on the range and on the course. It tells you what to work on, not what you came for.

The relationship

A real pro isn't two products.

He teaches you on the range and then tells you what to hit on the seventh tee. Same person, same judgment, same method. Software usually splits those into a coaching app and a caddie app. This doesn't.

On the range

Diagnose and prescribe

You film your swings. He names the fault in his own words and prescribes the fix he'd give someone built like you, with the practice time you actually have. Then he retests you to see whether it moved.

On the course

Know the hole and know you

He knows the hole in front of you and he knows you miss right with a driver under pressure. So he tells you 3-wood, left half, take the bogey — the same thing he'd say standing next to you.

The only reason this works is that both halves read from the same profile. Most golf apps know your swing or your scores. Neither knows both, and nobody has a professional's judgment sitting between them.

Assessment

Eight shots. Three swings each. Unedited.

Self-reported handicap is the weakest number in golf. So level comes from video instead — a fixed protocol, so that six months from now the progress you see is you improving rather than your camera moving. Three swings because everybody submits their one good one, and the spread across three says more than the best of them.

Before you play

He preps the round before you tee off.

You tell him where you're playing Saturday. He opens the card, sees every hole next to your own history on it, and writes the plan in advance. Pick a hole.

The directory

Every state. Then every course.

Country to state to course to hole, all of it clickable. Somewhere between 33,000 and 38,000 courses exist worldwide depending on who's counting, and roughly 16,000 of them are in the United States. Course coverage isn't the product — the coaching is. It's what makes the product feel like it has no edges.

What this is

A demo, and nothing more than that.

Real

  • The design spec and three implementation plans in this repository.

Not real yet

  • Every number on the scorecard. Coyote Wash is invented.
  • The level readout. No golfer has been assessed.
  • The prep notes. Written to show the voice, not by the pro.
  • All of the software. None of it is built.

Course geometry will come from OpenStreetMap under ODbL, and American course imagery from USDA NAIP, which is public domain. Nothing is scraped from commercial golf directories. Legal copy stays marked as draft until an attorney clears it.
