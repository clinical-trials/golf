New-golfer FAQ

The questions everyone has and nobody asks.

What do I wear?
Wear this
  • Collared or golf-appropriate top
  • Tailored shorts, pants, skort or golf dress
  • Golf shoes or clean athletic shoes
Skip this
  • Denim or gym shorts
  • Tank tops or graphic tees
  • Flip-flops or sandals

Safe rule: clean and neat — a collared or golf-appropriate top, tailored shorts, pants, skort or golf dress, and golf or clean athletic shoes. Skip denim, gym shorts, tank tops and flip-flops. Private clubs run more formal (shirt tucked, belt on, check their dress code first); public courses and simulator lounges are more relaxed. One bold piece is fine — the deeper etiquette is effort: look intentional and course-appropriate. You don’t need expensive clothes.

For women: a collared or sleeveless-collared polo, mock-neck or quarter-zip — worn with a skort, golf skirt, tailored shorts, golf pants or leggings, or a golf dress for one-and-done. Finish with a visor or cap, sunglasses and spikeless golf shoes (or clean white sneakers). Skorts and dresses are the most popular for a reason; at private clubs keep shorts and skirts around mid-thigh and check whether leggings need a skort over them. Pastels, brights and patterns are all welcome.

For men: a collared polo with tailored shorts or golf pants and a belt; add a cap and golf shoes. That combination clears almost any dress code in the game.

Do I need my own clubs?

No. Rentals can be expensive (especially if they’re any good) and borrowed clubs (pitching wedge, putter, 7 iron, and 5 wood) are how most golfers start, and our First 90 Days program assumes exactly that. Buy clubs around week 10, once you know your distances — or start with a fitted starter kit when you’re ready. In some cases you only need a single club to get going — a 7 iron alone can do it.

Am I good enough to play a real course?

Yes. Start at a pitch-and-putt or par-3 course, play at quiet times, use forward tees, and pick up when a hole goes sideways. Nobody in golf cares about your score — only your pace and safety.

What’s the etiquette I actually need?

Be polite. Be patient. Shake hands at the end of a game (men remove hats, ladies do not have to but can). Four things cover 90%: be quiet and still while others hit, stand safely to the side, keep pace (be ready on your turn), and fix your ball marks and divots. If you are falling behind your playing partners or the group in front of you, pick up and go to the next hole. Yell “Fore!” loudly if a ball might reach someone. Everything else you’ll pick up by playing.

How do the sessions work?

Fill out a quick questionnaire — your past experience, your equipment, and any physical limitations. You’ll get a template on how to film yourself for practice: send in one clip from down the line (toward the target) and one face-on — the video walks you through exactly how. Then your session, in person or virtual, is one-on-one with Tom: fire away with questions and start to understand the root causes of the most common shots. You’ll leave with 2–3 drills (the homework) to help you feel and understand the concepts that improve your game.
