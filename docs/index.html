New-golfer FAQ

The questions everyone has and nobody asks.

What do I wear?

Safe rule: clean and neat — a collared or golf-appropriate top, tailored shorts, pants, skort or golf dress, and golf or clean athletic shoes. Skip denim, gym shorts, tank tops and flip-flops. Private clubs run more formal (shirt tucked, belt on, check their dress code first); public courses and simulator lounges are looser. One bold piece is fine — the deeper etiquette is effort: look intentional and course-appropriate. You don’t need expensive clothes.

Do I need my own clubs?

No. Rentals and borrowed clubs are how most golfers start, and our First 90 Days program assumes exactly that. Buy clubs around week 10, once you know your distances — or start with a fitted starter kit when you’re ready.

Am I good enough to play a real course?

Yes. Start at a pitch-and-putt or par-3 course, play at quiet times, use forward tees, and pick up when a hole goes sideways. Nobody in golf cares about your score — only your pace and safety.

What’s the etiquette I actually need?

Five things cover 90%: be quiet and still while others hit, stand safely to the side, keep pace (be ready on your turn), fix your ball marks and divots, and count every stroke. Yell “Fore!” loudly if a ball might reach someone. Everything else you’ll pick up by playing.

How do the classes work?

Flipped classroom: watch a short lesson at home, do a focused piece of homework at the range or a short course, then take a quick low-stakes quiz — instant feedback, no grades. Five programs cover beginners, women, families and improvers.
