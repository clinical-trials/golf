Swing mechanics

See your swing the way Tom does.

Film your swing with your phone — markerless motion capture, no sensors and no suit. It reads the fundamentals Tom coaches: posture, sequence, low point, face and path — and tracks them over time so you can see change, not just feel it. In person, Tom pairs it with Swing Catalyst, force plates, a launch monitor and SAM PuttLab. At a simulator or the range, the app records the instant you swing, loops the replay right there beside your ball data, and submits it for Tom’s diagnosis and a corrective plan.

The standard capture

Eight shots, three swings each

Driver and a mid-iron from two angles, a pitch, a chip and two putting views — the same protocol every time, so a month from now the difference is you improving, not the camera moving.

New-golfer FAQ

The questions everyone has and nobody asks.

What do I wear?
Wear this
  • Collared or golf-appropriate top
  • Tailored shorts, pants, skort or golf dress
  • Golf shoes or clean athletic shoes
Skip this
  • Denim or gym shorts
  • Tank tops or graphic tees
  • Flip-flops or sandals

Safe rule: clean and neat — a collared or golf-appropriate top, tailored shorts, pants, skort or golf dress, and golf or clean athletic shoes. Skip denim, gym shorts, tank tops and flip-flops. Private clubs run more formal (shirt tucked, belt on, check their dress code first); public courses and simulator lounges are more relaxed. One bold piece is fine — the deeper etiquette is effort: look intentional and course-appropriate. You don’t need expensive clothes.

For women: a collared or sleeveless-collared polo, mock-neck or quarter-zip — worn with a skort, golf skirt, tailored shorts, golf pants or leggings, or a golf dress for one-and-done. Finish with a visor or cap, sunglasses and spikeless golf shoes (or clean white sneakers). Skorts and dresses are the most popular for a reason; at private clubs keep shorts and skirts around mid-thigh and check whether leggings need a skort over them. Pastels, brights and patterns are all welcome.

For men: a collared polo with tailored shorts or golf pants and a belt; add a cap and golf shoes. That combination clears almost any dress code in the game.

Do I need my own clubs?

No. Rentals and borrowed clubs are how most golfers start, and our First 90 Days program assumes exactly that. Buy clubs around week 10, once you know your distances — or start with a fitted starter kit when you’re ready.

Am I good enough to play a real course?

Yes. Start at a pitch-and-putt or par-3 course, play at quiet times, use forward tees, and pick up when a hole goes sideways. Nobody in golf cares about your score — only your pace and safety.

What’s the etiquette I actually need?

Five things cover 90%: be quiet and still while others hit, stand safely to the side, keep pace (be ready on your turn), fix your ball marks and divots, and count every stroke. Yell “Fore!” loudly if a ball might reach someone. Everything else you’ll pick up by playing.

How do the classes work?

Flipped classroom: watch a short lesson at home, do a focused piece of homework at the range or a short course, then take a quick low-stakes quiz — instant feedback, no grades. Five programs cover beginners, women, families and improvers.
